# Fracturing Points

> *"The moment everything changed wasn't loud. It was quiet — like a seam in the world had been pulled loose, and nothing on either side of it quite fit together anymore."*

---

## About This Book

**Fracturing Points** is a non-fiction exploration of what happens to a person's reality — their sense of self, their memory, and their perception of the world — when they are subjected to extreme emotional distress, life-threatening events, or deeply traumatic experiences.

This book grew out of something that happened to my father. After an event that shattered his sense of safety and identity, he began to notice things that shouldn't have been different — objects out of place, conversations no one else remembered, familiar rooms that felt subtly *wrong*. He came to believe that the event had not merely changed him. It had shifted him into a reality that was almost identical to the one he knew, but not quite.

Not the same. Never quite the same again.

This book is his story. It is also a theory about what happens at the edges of human endurance — and what it means when the edge gives way.

---

## The Core Concept

When a human being experiences an event of sufficient emotional magnitude — a near-death experience, profound loss, psychological crisis, or severe trauma — the mind undergoes fractures. These are not metaphorical. They manifest as real, lived disruptions in a person's experience of reality:

- **Objects go missing** that the person is certain were there.
- **Conversations are not remembered** by others who were present.
- **Places feel unfamiliar** despite being objectively unchanged.
- **The self becomes uncertain** — who am I, what do I want, what do I know?

The theory at the heart of this book is that these experiences are not signs of madness. They are signs of fracture.

Each fracture is a shift — a transfer into a reality that is nearly identical to the one left behind, but fractionally different. The more fractures a person accumulates, the greater the drift. At some point, the distance between who they were and where they now find themselves becomes impossible to measure.

## Author's Note

This is a personal book. It comes from love for my father and from a need to understand what happened to him — and, by extension, to understand what happens to all of us when reality stops being reliable.

If you have experienced something that made the world feel wrong in ways you couldn't explain, this book is for you.

You are not losing your mind. You are standing at a fracturing point.
